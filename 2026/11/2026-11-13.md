## The Steam Cyberdeck: a couch lap tray for the Deck and a keyboard

A lap tray was designed in OpenSCAD (unprinted) so the handheld console and a Bluetooth keyboard can be used together on the couch without holding the console up in front of the face. A 65% keyboard well sits in front, with the console in a cradle behind it at the same ~25° lean as the existing desk stand, and pointing stays on the console's own trackpads and sticks. With no calipers on hand, dimensions were estimated from photos with a ruler in frame and the 19.05 mm key pitch as a scale. The tray comes out around 325 mm wide, too big for the 235 mm bed, so it splits into two halves joined by printed bowtie (butterfly) keys, and the cradle pins across the seam with 6 mm dowels and lifts off to double as a standalone desk stand. The cradle prints first as the angle and slot test. Two of the build's own checks caught bugs before export: overlapping joint pockets, and a maker's mark that had ended up buried inside the part. The mark is the standard debossed Boyo Labs chip on the cradle's back. Three ruler checks (keyboard width and depth, and the console's bottom-edge thickness in its case) and the cradle test print remain before the tray halves get printed.
