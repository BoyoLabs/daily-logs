## A 3MF with a hidden negative part

A downloaded Bambu Studio 3MF of a wrench-style tool was converted to STL. The catch was that the file held its engraved text as a "negative part." A naive mesh merge would have printed that text as solid raised lettering, so it was subtracted with a headless Blender boolean instead. Blender's exact solver with self-intersection handling turned on used up all the box's RAM and was killed by the out-of-memory killer, with nothing else affected. With that option off, it finished in seconds.

## A two-colour gift sign, done as inlays

A new two-colour gift sign was built: a parody of a big-box store wordmark with a snowmobile silhouette in place of the logo mark. It moves away from the stencil-over-backing method used on an earlier sign to true inlays. Shallow pockets go into a thick base, and the second-colour letters and silhouette are printed separately and glued in flush, so no stencil bridges are needed. The sign is split into three tiles, with the seams routed only through gaps so every inlay stays one piece. All geometry checks pass on the rendered output, and a watermark in the source clipart dropped out on its own at the trace threshold. The recessed Boyo Labs maker's mark was carried over to the back of the end tile. That turned up a small gotcha: OpenSCAD writes no SVG at all for an empty 2D export, so the checker now treats a missing file as an empty shape.

## Why a string over a nail won't hang a wide sign level

A single string sliding freely over a nail can't hold a wide two-hole sign level. To be stable, the centre of mass has to sit farther below the holes than c²/b, which is about 500 mm for this sign. It's the same reason wire-hung pictures drift. The fix is in how it's hung, not in the geometry: tie a loop at the string's midpoint so the nail becomes a fixed pivot. The holes are placed symmetrically about the modelled centre of mass, and the worst-case tilt across 10–100% infill is 0.4°. The sign is now waiting on a colour choice and a test print of one tile to check the inlay fit.
