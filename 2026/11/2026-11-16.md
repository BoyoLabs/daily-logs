# 2026-11-16

## A circuit-board coaster set that tiles into one board

A four-piece Boyo Labs coaster set was designed in OpenSCAD. Each 96 mm square coaster is styled as a small circuit board, with debossed traces, via rings and corner mounting holes, and each has its own centrepiece: the full traced chip logo, a BOYO/LABS wordmark on an IC package, the simplified chip mark scaled up, and an Erlenmeyer flask drawn in traces. Every edge carries pads at the same mirrored positions, so the four tiles join into one continuous board in any 2x2 order or rotation. The art is recessed 0.8 mm so a filament swap at the right layer gives a two-colour "copper on green" look on a single-nozzle printer, and the traced logo's stencil slits were closed morphologically to keep every feature above the 0.9 mm printable floor. Unprinted.

## A "J1" socket to hold them

A matching holder was added: an open-top tray styled as J1, the socket the four chips plug into, with debossed package legs on its side walls. It has 0.75 mm of clearance per side, the top coaster sits 4 mm proud of the rim, U-shaped finger notches front and back let you pinch the stack out, and eight via-style holes in the floor let damp coasters air out. It prints as a single part; a separate fit-test ring was dropped. Unprinted.

## The cyberdeck drawer, back as a bolt-on

A day after a built-in drawer was ruled out for the lap tray, the idea came back in a different shape: a drawer that hangs under the existing tray as a separate add-on, with its front doubling as a wrist rest. It welds onto the current build rather than forcing a reprint. The tray floor becomes the drawer's ceiling, and the cradle's existing dowel holes double as alignment. The wrist-rest height has to match the keyboard's front edge, so design waits on measurements.
