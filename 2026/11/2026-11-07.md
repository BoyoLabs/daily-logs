# 2026-11-07

## The keycap logo, redrawn for the nozzle

The first Boyo Labs Esc keycap printed with a perfect stem fit at the default clearance, but its full chip logo smeared exactly as predicted. At keycap size its pins and letter slits were 0.15–0.45 mm, below what a 0.4 mm nozzle can draw. Instead of tuning slicer settings, the logo was redrawn in real millimetres with a 0.9 mm feature floor (two extrusion lines) for every raised and sunken detail: four pins per side instead of ten, a solid B and L, and the letters kept as islands inside the chip. A morphological check confirmed nothing thinner than the floor survived, and a control run with a bigger probe proved the check was sensitive. The simplified logo became a reusable module, and three designs used it the same day.

## A lighter sleeve that prints flat

A branded grip sleeve for a full-size BIC lighter was designed in OpenSCAD, with a small foot at the base. The reference design angled that foot down off a corner, which would need supports and leaves nothing flat to stand on, so the foot's underside was put in the same plane as the base. The part prints upright with no supports, and the foot's face is the smooth first layer. The fit reuses the coozie's crush-rib idea in an elliptical bore: eight round ribs touch the lighter, and only their tips set the fit.

## Two missed prints that bracketed the fit

The first print was loose. On video the lighter lifted straight out with no drag and tipped in the bore. That was first blamed on the lighter being smaller than its retail size, and a four-ring fit ladder was generated to find the right value. The second full print, tightened, jammed partway in and needed pliers. Read together, the two misses reversed the first diagnosis: a lighter at the retail size predicts both results, about 0.2 mm per side of slop and then about 0.25 mm of interference on rigid ribs. So the size was right and the usable window is narrow. Where it jammed, partway in rather than at the mouth, exposed a second flaw: full-length ribs make friction grow with every millimetre of insertion. The third version sets the rib tips exactly at the lighter's size and cuts the ribs down to two short bands, one at the floor and one under the lip. That's about a quarter of the contact, while the spacing between the bands still stops the lighter tipping.

Along the way, the debossed wordmark twice came out of the CAD kernel with a few non-manifold edges. The first time, two letters touched; the second time, letter baselines landed exactly on the curved skin's facets, and a fraction-of-a-millimetre sideways shift fixed it. The build now runs a mesh check on every output, so it can't slip through quietly.

## A hidden maker's mark on a shop sign

A recessed Boyo Labs maker's mark (the simplified chip logo plus a stacked "BOYO / LABS") was added to the back of one tile of a tiled shop sign, invisible once the sign hangs. That face prints against the bed, where first-layer squish eats into thin recesses, so the 0.9 mm floor mattered more than usual. Its clearance from the sign's other features is now an assertion that runs on every build, and the rebuild changed only that one file. The others came out byte-identical.
