# 2026-11-15

## The Steam Cyberdeck lap tray, printed and working at v1

The couch lap tray designed on 11/13 for the Steam Deck and a keyboard was printed and assembled, and it came out better than expected. It went straight to print with no rebuild even though no calipers were on hand: the fit-critical dimensions had been estimated from photos, using a ruler in frame and the known key pitch of the keyboard as scale references, and they held up. The two tray halves are joined with printed bowtie keys and dowels, glued and plastic-welded. Photos of the finished unit show the Deck's touchpads and sticks sitting fully above the keyboard in the raised cradle, and the keyboard fills its well with no visible play.

## First real session from the cyberdeck

The first working session was driven entirely from the finished unit, through a small Deck-side app built on the Deck itself that fronts the agent-console web frontend and reaches the home server remotely. That made the session the first real-world run of the Deck app as well as the first test of the tray. The keyboard works great and the Deck's built-in touchpads work well as the pointer. The touchpad is the one place with ergonomic room left, but it's acceptable and about as good as the form factor allows, so it reads as a limit of a lap tray rather than a flaw in v1. The practical result is that the cyberdeck covers day-to-day lab work on its own, with no need to go back to the desktop.

## A built-in storage drawer, considered and ruled out

A storage drawer in the tray was considered and dropped. It would add thickness and bulk, and the tray is mainly used reclined, where a thin profile matters more than on-board storage. The next step is a testing period to see whether any dimensional changes turn out to be needed.
