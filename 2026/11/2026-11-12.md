# 2026-11-12

## Dashboard chart fix and a ledger backfill

The 1Y chart on the income dashboard had a steep ramp at its left edge, and the cause was deeper than the first fix assumed. The charts space points by count, not by time, and older history is one point per day while recent history is every 5 minutes, so the first four weeks were squeezed into a sliver of the width. Thinning the 1Y view to one point per day, across all five chart views, fixed it, verified with headless-browser screenshots.

A distribution ledger in the same app was also backfilled. The brokerage's tools have no payment-history endpoint, so the sources were the fund sponsors' own published notices (which parsed cleanly as PDFs) plus the brokerage's dividend-schedule feed. A few rows had pay dates off by a day or several, and those were corrected. Every backfilled row is labeled computed-not-statement. The app only had an add path for that table, so corrections were made directly in the database after taking a copy first.

## Experimenting with multi-agent teams

A day of experimenting with how several AI agents can work together on lab questions. Each agent got a narrow brief and read-only defaults, and a coordinating session ran them as parallel background subagents and merged their reports. The most useful result was about format: independent first-round pitches followed by a second round where every agent saw all the others' pitches, voted, and pushed back produced real cross-talk, while a single round mostly produced agreement. In the second round, ownership questions got settled by reading code rather than guessing, one agent withdrew a claim when challenged, and a deliberately skeptical agent rated its own case against the consensus at about 4 out of 10. The agents also mostly argued against adding more agents, and the one gap they agreed on was ruled out at current volume. How often to run this kind of session is still undecided, and weekly or on-demand looks like a better fit than daily.

## Backups simplified

A side finding from one of those sessions was that the nightly USB copy had been healthy all along, and the overnight error came from the cloud-upload leg failing to see an archive it had just uploaded, a shared-API-client problem that was already ticketed. The off-site cloud upload (failing most nights on rate limits and a path error) and the daily backup email (the snapshot had outgrown the mail size cap and had been bouncing for two weeks) were both retired. They were disabled rather than deleted so either can return, so backups are now local plus a USB copy. The agent configuration files had never been in the backup set, and now are.

## A bull-vs-bear desk scene

A multi-agent design vote clustered ideas into five concepts and landed 6 to 2 for a maker's-mark stamp set, with the dissent's durability worry folded into the winning pitch as a printed master plus a registration jig and a proof strike. A server-status plate (two of its jobs had just been retired) was ruled out.

The actual design that day was a bull-vs-bear desk scene instead. A pure OpenSCAD version built from hulled spheres read as toy blobs, so it was rebuilt as a hybrid: the animals come from a scripted Blender metaball model so body parts blend like clay, while OpenSCAD keeps the precise oval plinth and the recessed maker's mark and unions everything into one solid. Gotchas worth keeping: a lone metaball's surface sits at about 0.57x its radius; the "socket" at each horn root that looked like a hole in renders turned out to be a concave fillet, confirmed with a geometric containment check; and the underside mark has to be mirrored on the axis that matches how people actually flip the piece over.
