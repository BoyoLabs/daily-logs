# 2026-10-01

## Ironman gets a farm: third area, first timer-based skill

Ironman, the offline OSRS-style RPG, shipped v0.7.0 "Windfall" — its third area, and the first whose design was handed over entirely ("surprise me"). It's a farm, with a new Farming skill that is the game's first timer skill rather than a dice-roll one: plant six beds, they grow on the game clock even while you're in another area, then harvest. Seeds were considered and dropped, since every tier would need a source and the shops only stock the two starter tiers — it would either break the rule that lower tiers never become unreachable or need a second drop system. The waiting is what makes the older skills trainable there, via a pond, timber stands, a second cooking range and three new monsters.

The area's boss, Johnny Appleseed, is the third in the tall-tale line and the first ranged one (he throws apples, and is weak to melee), so the three bosses now cover the whole combat triangle. Three rare drops roll separately — armour, a skilling perk and a weapon — giving one of each kind of reward rather than three of one. Most of the area's art turned out to already be in the repo unused (a farm asset pack with crops, livestock and a barn had only been supplying two rocks), so only the boss, scarecrow, gate and drops were hand-pixeled.

A new 48-check browser suite and the 11 older suites all pass; three older checks had hard-coded the previous area's name as the game's title and were updated. Balance is hand-computed, not play-tested.

## Text-only 3D generation, tested on a toy: "okay, not great"

A frog flipper toy (press the tail tab, finger slides off, frog hops) was designed in OpenSCAD as a test of text-only 3D generation with the current model, and published to the file server's prints folder. The key decision was modeling it as a side silhouette extruded across its width and printed lying on its side, which makes the tail tab a plain vertical wall with no supports and runs the layer lines along the tab's bend direction so the flexure doesn't snap at a layer line. The cost is that the top view is a chamfered slab rather than a sculpted frog. It technically meets the brief but isn't what was pictured, so the verdict was "okay, not great": this approach looks more promising for practical, functional parts than characterful toys. No further iterations, and it wasn't printed.
