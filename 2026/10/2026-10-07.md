## A slicer project converted to a plain STL without losing its engraving

A slicer project file for a small two-ended wrench-style tool was converted to a plain STL and put on the file server alongside its source. The project turned out not to be one mesh but an assembly of seven solid parts plus one "negative part" — engraved lettering the slicer subtracts at slice time — and since STL has no concept of negative volume, a plain merge would have turned the engraving into raised text. The conversion was done in two stages instead: a small parser that applies each part's placement and tags it solid or cutter, then a real union-then-difference in an exact boolean solver. The result is watertight, the removed volume matches the embedded depth of the text block exactly, and a render shows the lettering cut in and reading the right way round. Not yet printed.

## A no-drill pendant attachment for hardened steel

A pendant attachment was designed for hardened mower-blade steel that avoids drilling altogether: two side notches form a narrow neck near the top, and a cord or wire wrap seats there. Hardened steel makes drilling slow, and a hole near an edge is a crack starter. A cross template at true scale and a printable fit-test of it were built, along with a notched-neck variant of the existing Mjolnir pendant template. The decision that came out of it: the plain version for steel, with the rune groove kept only for printed pieces.
