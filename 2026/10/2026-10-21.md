# 2026-10-21

## Woodsman Ways hat patches become a stencil line

Two printed leather hat-patch stencils were drafted: a long strip for the front of the main Woodsman Ways hats with the logo and the stacked WOODSMAN/WAYS lockup, and a small square logo-only tag for other hats. Each stencil does three jobs at once: it's the cut outline, the rivet marks, and the pen-through openings for pyrography. The lettering has stencil bridges so letters like O, D, and A don't drop their centers, and it's thickened slightly so the thin W diagonals still print and take a pen. Since many more saying and hobby strips are planned, this became a small pipeline. There's a shared OpenSCAD library, a template, one script to start a new patch, and one to build it and run checks: no loose islands in the plate and no artwork under a rivet cap. The stencil alphabet is now generated automatically with bridges placed for you. An alphabet test caught that thickening the letters sealed the serif gap in E and F into a loose sliver, which was fixed by allowing sideways bridges that reopen the font's own gap.

## First saying strip: "Fairways & Waterways"

The first saying strip pairs golf and fishing, "FAIRWAYS & WATERWAYS", which echoes the brand name. It has a golf flag icon on the left and a fish on the right. The flag worked on the first try. The fish took several rounds. The reference image offered for a leaping bass turned out to be a watermarked commercial design, so it wasn't traced, and an original leaping largemouth was drawn instead. Formula-driven shapes came out clunky, while hand-placed control points with corner-preserving smoothing worked. Fins have to sweep back, a pinched throat makes the head read as a bird's, and everything has to be judged at true scale. The verdict was "closer, not quite there", but usable, and the fish will likely be finished by hand during burning. Organic clipart at about 24 mm is the weak spot of the stencil pipeline. Text, logos, and simple icons land easily.

## A new rule for leather patterns: straight lines, chamfered corners

New leather patterns use straight lines wherever possible, and corners get a roughly 45° chamfer instead of a rounded radius. A straight cut is one pass along a straightedge, so pieces come out consistent, while curves are cut freehand and vary. A draft can still be rounded internally, but it gets converted to straight cuts that hug the curve before anyone sees it. The hat patches were retrofitted the same day. The chamfer is tangent to the old corner radius rather than a chord across it, so it stays within about half a millimeter of the old curve and keeps the full margin of leather around each rivet. One library change rebuilt all three patches.

## A cast iron skillet handle sleeve

The next leather pattern is a riveted handle sleeve for cast iron skillets, printed as one stencil. Bags and general shop items were considered, but a country, rugged direction was the one that clicked. The sleeve is one piece folded lengthwise, so one side of the handle is seamless and the rivets run only along a flat fin on the other side and across the closed tip. The rivets sit beside the handle, never on the grip faces, and a margin keeps every rivet post off the hot iron. It's sized so the mouth slides over the widest point of the biggest handle, then wet-formed to the taper. The stencil went to the printer, and the leather build and fit check are still to come.

## A second way to estimate income in the portfolio app

The portfolio app's Income stats can now estimate monthly and daily income two ways, chosen with a toggle that's remembered per browser. The original method is shares times each fund's latest payout. The new one is each fund's distribution rate times what the position is worth right now, so it moves with price. The catch is that if the rate is measured against today's price, the math reduces to exactly the old method. So the rate is measured against the closing price on the payout's ex-date, which is fetched once per payout and cached. The two methods came out close on day one, and the gap will grow as prices drift between payouts.

## Agent console: phone tweaks and a login-expiry warning

On phones, the agent console's mic key on the prompt line is now a file-upload key, since uploads matter more than dictation on mobile. The mic comes back automatically while hands-free voice mode is running, because there it's the status display and the barge-in control. The console also gained a login-expiry warning that copies the CLI's own rule, found by reading the CLI's code: warn once the stored login has less than three days left. It shows as an amber strip pointing to the terminal for renewal, since the console can't run the interactive login itself. Every tab also got its own close button, so tabs can be closed from anywhere.
