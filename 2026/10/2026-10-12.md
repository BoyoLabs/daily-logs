# 2026-10-12

## New-IP email alerts switched off

The alert that fired whenever a never-seen IP touched a login gate was turned off across every gated service. In practice nearly all of them were the owner's own phone picking up a fresh carrier address, so it was noise with no way to learn its way out. It was done as a single kill switch in the one shared helper rather than unwiring each app, and brute-force lockout and geo-blocking are untouched, since the alert only ever reported and never blocked.

## A macro catch-up email

A weekday pre-open brief now arrives by email: the day's scheduled US economic events, a cross-asset snapshot (index futures, VIX, crude, the 10-year, the dollar, gold), then the headlines driving the indexes grouped by topic. An intraday alert sits beside it and stays silent unless a cross-asset move crosses a deliberately wide step. "Big" is defined by the market moving, not by a model judging headlines, because a news-only trigger would fire every day.

## A small local model demoted from writing to selecting

The first version had a small local model write the "what happened / why it matters" summary. On the same 60 real headlines, one model ignored the JSON schema entirely and the other read "oil surge keeps S&P 500, Nasdaq in check" and reported that oil was helping stabilise stocks, which is the opposite. Model-written prose was dropped the same day: the model now only picks headlines under a fixed topic list and the email quotes them verbatim, so the worst it can do is pick a dull one, and the calendar and prices never touch it. Two more catches came out of testing: a run that spent all eight slots on near-identical oil headlines, fixed with a per-topic cap enforced in code, and a run that generated for the full timeout because constrained-JSON output can loop on "one more array item", fixed with a hard output-length cap and bounded arrays.

## An animated football gamecast on the demo hub

A new demo draws a top-down football field driven by ordinary public play-by-play data: every game of the week, live or replayed from the opening kickoff, with all 22 players, a scoreboard, commentary, box score, win-probability chart and highlight clips, plus a red-zone view that tiles every live game inside the 20. The public feed says what happened on a play but not where each player ran, so ball spots, yardage, results and the named players are real while everyone's path is staged to fit those facts, and the page says so. It is one self-contained client-side page, so the visitor's browser pulls the feed directly and the server proxies and stores nothing. It was verified by building every play of all 15 games of the week, about 2,700 plays, with zero build errors; the live multi-game split has only been checked with a test hook, since one game was live during the build.

## Filling the dead air between plays

Watching a live game showed the field frozen for 20 to 40 seconds between plays, so a between-plays layer was added: a huddle and line-up when the same team keeps the ball, both units jogging off on a change of possession, and a rotating stats card at timeouts and quarter breaks. A locally ticking game clock was ruled out for live games, because the page runs behind the broadcast and cannot know when the real clock stopped. Finished games are different, since both ends of every gap are known, so they now replay at a "broadcast pace" by default with the clock gliding from one play to the next and landing exact, about 42 minutes a game against roughly 15 before. The same round fixed a scoreboard that read "a play behind" between plays and kept every credited player's name under his dot for the whole play.
