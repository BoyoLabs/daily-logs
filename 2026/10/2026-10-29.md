## A shop sign stencil, and how a likeness survives a stencil

A roughly 27-inch pyrography shop sign was drafted from a description and a photo: a portrait medallion between two words, split into four printable tiles that are plastic-welded together, with seams placed only in clean gaps between letters. The portrait took three tries. Fine line work read as a shaggy creature, and a three-tone stipple read as noise, before a bold two-tone woodcut with about 18 shapes read at a glance. The lesson: at stencil scale, a likeness comes from a few big, separated shapes, and detail from the photo works against it.
