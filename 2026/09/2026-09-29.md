# 2026-09-29

## Oakhollow: a second area, two new skills, and a second boss (v0.5.0–v0.6.0)

First multi-zone build: a whirlpool on the Riverside river now drags players into an island boss instance against Fisherman Bob, sized to combat level in buckets of five per the "tiers, not a mirror" rule, with a 1/25 drop — a fishing rod that doubles as a two-handed melee weapon hitting from three tiles away, gated by Fishing level. A minimap and a world map generated straight from zone data arrived alongside it, plus original chiptune music synthesized in the browser, so no audio files and no licensing question. A standing rule followed: every area gets an obvious travel portal beside its spawn point, enforced by a test across all zone files, with boss/instance zones explicitly excluded and kept on their own themed exits. Then came Oakhollow, a lumberjack camp and the game's second area (it renames itself after its newest location), adding Woodcutting and Firemaking — fires double as portable cooking ranges — three new monsters, a combat-level-10 entry gate, and a second boss, Paul Bunyan, guarding rare drops including full armor and a faster-swinging thrown axe. A design principle landed here too: in-game prompts never reveal rare drops, but the optional update log does, doubling as build history.

## Playtest catches, a trader economy, and a feel pass (v0.6.1–v0.6.5)

Oakhollow got an on-theme trader who buys logs — a coin outlet for Woodcutting — and sells only starter-tier stock under the Ironman rule, plus his own campsite with a sittable, players-cookable campfire. The boss's rare thrown axe picked up a woodcutting perk: a chance at a bonus log with no extra XP, deliberately a yield perk rather than a speed one so it stays distinct from the fishing rod. The first real player-side rare drop (the rod) immediately surfaced a genuine bug — its signature bobber attack had never actually been drawn, because the automated tests checked the game event rather than the rendered frame. A clean case of human playtesting catching what green tests missed; the tests now check drawn projectiles too. A readability pass followed from playtest feedback: every multi-tick skilling action now gives visible per-tick feedback (swings, tree shake, sparks, smoke), a progress-ring badge, and a marker on choppable trees that had been indistinguishable from scenery.

## Serrated edge on a tape dispenser reprint

Added a serrated cutting edge along one side of the tape slit on a 50mm tape dispenser model that started life as a sourceless STL import — the teeth were unioned onto the import rather than the piece being re-modeled from scratch, wedge-shaped and tapering to a thin tip so they bite into packing tape while still printing support-free.
