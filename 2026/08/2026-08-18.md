## A new personal tool: clip and photo mashup editor

Built a new personal video tool from scratch: upload clips and photos (including iPhone photo formats), reorder them, pick a target length, and it auto-assembles a short mashup — aimed at a Monday/Thursday short-form posting habit. PIN-gated like the rest of the personal app suite and added to the dashboard and mobile glance view automatically. Iterated on it hands-on through the day based on real use: added the ability to delete a project, real upload and generation progress bars, and reworked the voiceover feature so it records live while watching the actual trimmed cut instead of being recorded separately — which also fixed a choppiness problem in that preview by having it play back one real assembled video instead of switching between separate clips in the browser. Also smoothed out the captions-step progress bar, which had been reading as stuck for long stretches before jumping.

## TikTok test decided a platform focus

Uploaded a generated video to TikTok and saw a small playback stutter; the same file played cleanly on YouTube Shorts. Decided to focus on YouTube Shorts going forward — TikTok's quality has felt like it's been declining, and YouTube's been getting better traction anyway — rather than chase TikTok-specific encoding fixes.

## A full-length landscape mode added, with its own voiceover editor

Leaning into the YouTube decision, added a second project format alongside the original short vertical mashups: a landscape, full-length mode with no length cap, where every clip plays in full back to back. Gave it a proper multi-part voiceover editor instead of one whole-video recording — the assembled video plays with normal scrub controls, hit record wherever the playhead is, stop whenever, and it shows up as its own block on a track underneath, so only the parts that need narration get one and the rest keeps its original sound. A block can be deleted from a list underneath, with re-recording just being delete-then-record-again at that spot. Also added a per-photo duration control for that mode, since photos need a screen time set explicitly while video clips just keep their own length. Rounded it out later in the day: uploading a photo now immediately offers to record a voiceover for just that photo, with its on-screen length set to match the recording automatically instead of needing to be dictated by hand, and both that photo recorder and the in-app camera recorder now play back a stopped recording with an approve-or-retry choice before it's added to the project, so a bad take can be discarded and redone.

## Video encoding offloaded to the idle GPU

Tried offloading the tool's video-encoding work to the discrete GPU normally reserved for the desktop stream, since it otherwise sits idle. That GPU has a documented history of freezing under hardware-encode workloads specifically, so it was tested cautiously in isolation first — a small clip, then a realistic job, then the specific caption-burn combination — and all three came back clean with the GPU still responsive afterward. Wired it in with an automatic fallback to normal CPU encoding if anything ever goes wrong.

## In-app camera recorder added

Added an in-app camera recorder as an option alongside uploading, with a button to flip between front and back camera. The first attempt at holding the phone in portrait while still producing a landscape-shaped clip rotated the footage, which got the right shape but left the subject looking sideways; fixed by center-cropping to the landscape frame instead, which keeps the subject upright at the cost of a narrower field of view — an actual tradeoff, not a bug.
