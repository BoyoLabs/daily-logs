# 2026-08-27

## A castle you can walk through

Stood up a new small demo-hosting surface to host a first-person browser
walkaround built from a 3D-printed STL — the Lumbridge Castle model, scaled
to human size in Three.js — complete with a floating "Boyo Labs presents"
title that always faces the camera, replacing an earlier ground-stencil
version of the same text.

## Chasing two "it's not showing up" reports

The first turned out to be a real camera-math bug: the floating title's
initial placement sat right at the edge of the camera's vertical field of
view from spawn, so it rendered but was effectively invisible unless you
looked up — fixed by repositioning off the actual FOV math and verified
with a fresh headless-browser screenshot. The second was browser/edge
caching on the demo's JS, confirmed by fetching straight from origin and
from a clean browser context.

## Mobile controls

Added touch controls to the walkaround — a virtual joystick for movement
and a drag-to-look surface — gated behind a touchscreen check so desktop
mouse/keyboard stayed untouched, with the mouse-look rotation math
reimplemented by hand for touch since the pointer-lock library only
reacts to real mouse movement events.

## A goblin that fights back

Converted a second STL — a goblin miniature holding two swords — into a
hittable enemy. Orthographic render probes revealed it had been printed
lying on its back, so a rotation fix went in, then a from-scratch heuristic
colored the model (it had no material data at all) by ray-casting each
face's local thickness to separate thin blade/spike geometry from the
thick body, since the swords turned out welded to the hands as one
connected mesh with no clean way to select them separately. He hops in
place, makes small runtime-synthesized sound effects, and can be attacked
— click on desktop, a dedicated on-screen button on mobile — with a
hit-flash, a blood-particle burst, and a death animation after enough
hits.

Also tidied up: moved the goblin's source STL out of a personal downloads
folder into the demo project's own build directory, alongside its
conversion script.
